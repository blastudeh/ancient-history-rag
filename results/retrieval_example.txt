In ancient Egypt, papyrus served as a writing material and as a resource for everyday objects. To make a writing surface, workers arranged strips from the stalk's inner pith in layers and dried them under pressure. Sheets could be joined into rolls. Egypt's dry conditions helped preserve papyrus documents. Surviving examples include letters, contracts, administrative records and religious texts.
